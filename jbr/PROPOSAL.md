## Executive proposition

JBR Global Discovery & Research Network (JBR-GDRN) is proposed as an international multidisciplinary research infrastructure that transforms important unresolved scientific questions into structured collaborative research programs.

The defining proposition is:

**Scientific problem → evidence mapping → research gap → multidisciplinary expertise map → international collaboration call → team formation → rigorous research → validation → reproducible discovery → publication / data / software / innovation → impact**

JBR is not intended to be only a journal, researcher directory or statistical-analysis portal. Its primary function is to create a professional mechanism through which researchers can share ideas, identify defensible research gaps, advertise approved multidisciplinary research challenges, recruit specialists internationally and perform coordinated research.

## Strategic objectives

1. Create a trusted international scientific identity and governance structure.
2. Provide a structured mechanism for submitting and reviewing new research ideas.
3. Convert promising gaps into JBR Discovery Challenges.
4. Define the exact expertise, methods and infrastructure required for each challenge.
5. Recruit collaborators transparently according to scientific specialization.
6. Support computational, experimental, clinical, field and integrative research.
7. Track project roles and contributions using transparent contributor principles.
8. Preserve reproducibility, provenance and version history where appropriate.
9. Disseminate validated outputs through peer-reviewed publications, datasets, software, protocols or innovation pathways.
10. Develop a long-term global research network spanning health, life science, agricultural, environmental and quantitative disciplines.

## Flagship product: JBR Discovery Challenge

Each approved challenge should define:
- scientific context
- knowledge gap
- central hypothesis
- anticipated novelty
- participating disciplines
- expertise required
- data requirements
- laboratory requirements
- computational requirements
- validation criteria
- ethics/IP considerations
- milestones
- expected outputs
- collaboration status and application route

## Platform modules

### Public layer
Institutional home, mission and vision, founders, scientific divisions, open Discovery Challenges, active and completed projects, public outputs, news, training and impact.

### Researcher layer
Identity/profile, expertise, ORCID/professional links, collaboration interests, challenge applications, project memberships and contribution history.

### Scientific review layer
Idea triage, literature/gap review, novelty assessment, feasibility assessment, multidisciplinary mapping, ethics/IP/data-governance review and challenge approval.

### Project layer
Private team workspace, roles, protocol, milestones, datasets, code, analysis, meeting records, figures, manuscripts, version history and output tracking.

### Data and methods layer
Approved data assets, methods, protocols, reproducibility records, analysis workflows and public research visualizations.

### Administration layer
Researcher verification, challenge publication, application review, project lifecycle, policy enforcement, audit events and platform analytics.

## Governance requirements

Before production research operations, JBR should formally approve:
- Research Integrity Policy
- Authorship and CRediT Policy
- Conflict of Interest Policy
- AI Use Policy
- Data Governance and Privacy Policy
- Human and Animal Research Rules
- Intellectual Property Policy
- Collaboration and Project Participation Terms
- Research Misconduct and Dispute Resolution Procedure
- Scientific Screening and Discovery Validation Standard

## Technical architecture

### Phase 1 — GitHub Pages MVP
Static responsive public portal, public Discovery Challenge catalogue, local functional forms, project documentation and backend schema.

### Phase 2 — Managed application backend
Supabase/PostgreSQL, authentication, verified researcher records, shared idea submissions, challenge applications, administrator review and role-based access.

### Phase 3 — Research workspace
Private projects, storage, milestones, document versioning, contribution tracking, analysis metadata and notifications.

### Phase 4 — Scientific intelligence
Responsible AI-assisted evidence mapping, potential gap organization, collaborator matching, method comparison, data-quality support and project intelligence.

### Phase 5 — Institutional scale
Dedicated domain, organization-owned infrastructure, secure cloud services, APIs, larger data/compute services, institutional partnerships, training and multinational research programs.

## Success criteria

A successful JBR platform should be able to demonstrate not merely account registrations, but:
- high-quality research questions submitted
- approved multidisciplinary challenges
- matched specialists from complementary disciplines
- active international project teams
- completed protocols and analyses
- independently validated findings
- peer-reviewed outputs
- reusable datasets/software where appropriate
- transparent contributor records
- measurable scientific or translational impact

## Founding mission

**To connect multidisciplinary scientific expertise across countries and institutions and transform unresolved research questions into rigorous, reproducible and impactful discoveries.**

## Founding vision

**To build an internationally trusted research ecosystem where researchers, technologies, datasets and disciplines converge to solve complex scientific problems that cannot be addressed effectively in isolation.**
