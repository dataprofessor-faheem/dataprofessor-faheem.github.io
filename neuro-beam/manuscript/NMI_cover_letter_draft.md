# Cover letter draft — Nature Machine Intelligence

Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript **“NEURO-BEAM: Explainable machine intelligence links neuronal bioelectric phenotypes to cross-disease genomic programs”** for consideration as an Article in *Nature Machine Intelligence*.

NEURO-BEAM addresses a methodological problem at the intersection of machine learning, computational neuroscience and genomics: how to integrate cellular physiology with molecular and disease-level genomic information without treating unmatched datasets as if they were same-cell multimodal observations. The framework learns electrophysiology–transcriptome relationships only from matched Patch-seq neurons, evaluates prediction with subject-grouped validation, freezes the model before independent motor-cortex testing, and projects the resulting molecular programs into separate human cancer cohorts through an auditable cross-domain transfer layer.

The study combines 3,654 matched visual-cortex Patch-seq neurons, external validation in 1,304 primary-motor-cortex neurons, a Top-500 pan-cancer–bioelectric discovery engine and an interpretable phenotype-conditioned membrane simulator. The strongest grouped-validation results reach Spearman correlations of approximately 0.91 for action-potential upstroke/downstroke ratio and 0.87 for membrane time constant, while frozen external models retain substantial rank concordance for several harmonized electrical phenotypes. All analysis outputs, provenance, downloadable matrices and integrity checks are exposed through a reproducible public research portal.

We believe the work is relevant to *Nature Machine Intelligence* because the primary contribution is a machine-intelligence architecture for scientific discovery: it combines leakage-safe multimodal learning, interpretable cross-domain projection, external validation and falsifiable simulation rather than presenting a web resource alone.

The manuscript does not claim that cancer mutations cause neuronal electrical phenotypes, and the dynamic simulator is explicitly labelled as association-conditioned rather than causal. These boundaries are built into both the analysis pipeline and the public interface.

Thank you for considering our work.
